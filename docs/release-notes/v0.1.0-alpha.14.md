**A consent terminal that goes away mid-run exits 3, where it used to exit 0** (#218). Under
`--approve call`, a terminal closed, hung up or answered with one typed end-of-input no longer
reads as an answer: the call asked about and every call behind it in that round are refused,
no further request is sent, and the account falls back to stderr. A caller branching on the
status has to tell 3 from 2, which still means a bound the operator chose cut the turn short.
`TurnOutcome` gained `round_stop` (#190), the provider's own stop reason for the round whose
text is the reply, so an embedder with no event observer can tell a turn cut off at
`max_tokens` from one that finished — the two produce byte-identical messages. `None` means no
round completed, and the figure is orthogonal to `TurnStop`: a turn out of rounds and cut in
its last round reports both. `agent-run` drops a line with it, a wrap-up round cut at
`--max-tokens` no longer reporting the cut.

**A granted directory replaced by another real one now refuses for every tool, not just
`bash`** (#212). The six that never spawn — `read`, `write`, `edit`, `ls`, `grep`, `find` —
compared names and re-resolved each granted spelling, so a real directory put at a granted
name was in bounds for them while `bash` refused the same substitution at the seam. Each
access now opens the granted spelling and confirms it still holds the `(dev, ino)` the grant
was vetted on; a mismatch refuses with `root_replaced` in the audit trail, and the orientation
prompt drops a root that no longer holds its object. `bash`'s `grant_redirected` and
`grant_replaced` change from a failure to a denial, so an agent is told to ask differently,
not to retry. The pin still cannot see an inode reused — a granted directory deleted and
re-created can compare equal — which `SECURITY.md` states as a non-claim.

**A `tui` subcommand draws a turn on a screen you can stop** (#133). It takes every
`agent-run` flag and bounds a tool call under the same policy; the answer, the reasoning and
each call's verdict are drawn in one pane as they arrive. Ctrl-c or escape ends the turn where
it stands, which **does not stop a tool**: a call already running finishes unseen (#26), and
nothing of that turn is stored, `--session` or not. The audit trail is held while the screen
owns the terminal and written to stderr once it is given back. It needs a terminal on stdout,
and refuses `--approve call`, whose question wants the terminal the screen has taken (#225).
