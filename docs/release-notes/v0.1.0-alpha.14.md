**A consent terminal that goes away mid-run exits 3, where it used to exit 0** (#218). Under
`--approve call`, a terminal that is closed, hung up or answered with one typed
end-of-input no longer reads as an answer: the call being asked about and every call behind
it in that round are refused, nothing after them runs, no further request is sent, and the
account of it falls back to stderr. A caller branching on the status has to tell 3 from 2,
which still means a bound the operator chose cut the turn short. Whatever the turn did
before that is on stdout and in `--session`.

**A turn's outcome says how its last round ended** (#190). `TurnOutcome` gained
`round_stop`, the provider's own stop reason for the round whose text is the reply, so an
embedder with no event observer can tell a turn cut off at `max_tokens` from one that
finished — the two produce byte-identical messages. Reported and not acted on: re-entry
still turns on the presence of tool calls, and the figure is orthogonal to `TurnStop`, so a
turn that ran out of rounds and was cut in its last round reports both. `None` means no
round completed. One line of `agent-run`'s output changes with it: a wrap-up round cut at
`--max-tokens` no longer reports the cut, the run having already said that round's text was
neither an answer nor saved.

**A `tui` subcommand draws a turn on a screen you can stop** (#133). It takes every
`agent-run` flag and bounds a tool call under the same policy; the answer, the reasoning and
each call's verdict are drawn in one pane as they arrive. Ctrl-c or escape ends the turn
where it stands, which **does not stop a tool**: a call already running finishes unseen
(#26), and nothing of that turn is stored, `--session` or not. It needs a terminal on
stdout, and refuses `--approve call`, whose question wants the terminal the screen has taken
(#225).
